Medicines: what Japan bans and what needs permission

This is where a trip to Japan can go wrong. Several medicines that are routine in the US are restricted in Japan, and one common ADHD medicine is banned outright.

MedicineIn JapanWhat to do
Adderall (amphetamine)Banned, even with a prescriptionTalk to your doctor about the trip well before you go
Vyvanse (lisdexamfetamine)Stimulants’ raw materialAdvance permission, applied for at least 14 days before you fly

Sources: the Narcotics Control Department (banned substances, permission, the 14-day lead time) and the Ministry of Health’s Q&A on bringing medicines into Japan (the one-month rule and Import Confirmation). This is not medical or legal advice, and the rules can change: those two pages decide, and both list an email address for questions.

Entry and arrival

US citizens don’t need a visa for a tourist stay: Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists the United States among the visa-exempt countries, with 90 days granted on arrival.

Register your immigration and customs details on Visit Japan Web before the flight and you get one QR code that covers both at the airport. Every traveler needs their own, children included. Visit Japan Web also works offline; its official manual shows how to set that up before you fly. The service is free, and Japan Customs warns about look-alike sites that charge for it. For customs it isn’t the only way: Japan’s tourism board says the paper customs declaration form is available on the plane and at the airport.

Plugs, power and getting around

  • Plugs. Japanese outlets take Type A plugs, two flat pins like most US plugs, and the power is 100 volts everywhere: 50 Hz in eastern Japan and 60 Hz in the west, including Nagoya, Kyoto and Osaka. A plug with a third, round pin needs an adapter; if one pin of your plug is wider than the other, bring an adapter to be safe. Phone and laptop chargers labeled 100–240 V work anywhere.
  • Suica on your iPhone. JR East’s Welcome Suica Mobile app issues a Suica and tops it up with a card in Apple Pay. Use it on trains, buses and in shops that show the IC logo. Some countries don’t allow issuing it before arrival; then set it up once you land.
  • Food in your bag. Japan’s tourism board says meat and meat products, plants and plant products can’t be brought in. That includes jerky.

Questions people ask

Do US citizens need a visa for Japan?

Not for a tourist stay. Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists the United States among the visa-exempt countries, with a 90-day period of stay granted on arrival.

Can I bring Adderall to Japan?

No. Japan’s Narcotics Control Department says amphetamine, the active ingredient in Adderall, cannot be brought into Japan even for treatment. Lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse) is allowed only with advance permission, applied for at least 14 days before you travel.

Can I bring Sudafed to Japan?

Pseudoephedrine, the decongestant in Sudafed, is on Japan’s list of stimulants’ raw materials, which need advance permission. Check your product with the Narcotics Control Department before you pack it.

Do I need Visit Japan Web?

For customs you don’t have to: Japan’s tourism board says the paper customs declaration form is available on the plane and at the airport. It saves time, though: you register your immigration and customs details before the flight, and one QR code covers both at the airport. Every traveler needs their own code. The service is free; Japan Customs warns about look-alike sites that charge a fee.

What plug does Japan use?

Type A, the two flat pins also used in the US, at 100 volts. Eastern Japan runs at 50 Hz and western Japan, including Nagoya, Kyoto and Osaka, at 60 Hz. A plug with a third, round grounding pin needs an adapter; if one pin of your plug is wider than the other, bring an adapter to be safe.
