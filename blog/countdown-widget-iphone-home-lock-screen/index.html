How to Put a Countdown on Your iPhone Lock Screen and Home Screen

Published 2026-09-26 · Updated 2026-10-18 · go ka

To put a countdown on your iPhone Lock Screen, set up the event in a countdown app that has widgets, then touch and hold the Lock Screen, tap Customize, choose the Lock Screen, tap Add Widgets under the clock, choose the countdown widget and tap Done. For the Home Screen, touch and hold an empty area, tap Edit and then Add Widget (the + button on iOS 17). In Countdown Widget: Any Event (Countdown) every widget size is free on both screens, with no limit on events.

Add a widget to your Lock Screen

Set up the event in the app first; the widget itself goes on from the Lock Screen, which iOS treats as a separate layout from your Home Screen:

  1. In Countdown, tap Add Event (or + once you already have events) and set the date you want to count down to.
  2. Touch and hold the Lock Screen until the Customize button appears at the bottom, then tap it.
  3. Of the two screens shown, choose the Lock Screen.
  4. Tap Add Widgets under the clock, or tap the widgets that are already there.
  5. Find Countdown in the list and choose a widget.
  6. Tap Done to save.

The same event can sit on both screens at once — they are independent widgets, not one shared placement, so you could keep a small tile on the Lock Screen for a glance and a larger one on the Home Screen for more detail.

Add a widget to your Home Screen

The steps are the same for any app with widgets; only the second step differs between iOS versions:

  1. Touch and hold an empty area of the Home Screen until the apps start to jiggle.
  2. On iOS 18 and later, tap Edit in the top-left corner, then Add Widget. On iOS 17, tap the + button in the top-left corner.
  3. Search for Countdown, tap it, then swipe through the size and style options.
  4. When you see the size you want, tap Add Widget, then drag it to where you want it.
  5. Tap Done to lock the layout back in.

If you change your mind about placement later, touch and hold the widget itself and choose Edit Home Screen, or drag it to a different spot while the icons are still jiggling. Removing a widget works the same way any app icon does — touch and hold, then tap Remove Widget — and it never deletes the event behind it, only the widget showing it.

Can you add a countdown widget without an app?

Not one that counts down days. iOS doesn’t come with a widget that shows how many days are left until a date you pick: the Calendar widget lists what’s coming up but not the days remaining, and the Clock app’s timer stops at 23 hours, 59 minutes and 59 seconds. A shortcut in the Shortcuts app can work out the days when you run it, and a daily automation can even redraw your wallpaper with a new number, but none of that puts the count in a widget.

So a number that updates by itself in a widget has to come from an app with a countdown widget. In Countdown every widget size is free and there is no limit on events, so it costs nothing to do this.

Make it bigger: the extra large widget in iOS 27

iOS 27 adds a new widget size above large. Apple's own summary is that extra-large widgets “let you see more information from your favorite apps right on the Home Screen”.

You don't have to go through the widget gallery to get it. Touch and hold the Countdown app icon, or a Countdown widget you already placed, and choose a size from the icons at the bottom of the menu; touch and hold again and tap the app icon button to turn it back into an icon. Not every app offers every size — Apple notes that some stop at small and medium — but Countdown offers all of them on iOS 27, extra large included, and none of them cost anything.

On iPhone Duo, Countdown is also laid out for the unfolded screen: your list and the event you're looking at sit side by side, and tapping a countdown on the left opens it on the right.

Which Countdown widget to choose

Countdown offers four widget types: one countdown, a list of the next few events coming up, a spotlight widget, and a tile for each event. Every widget size is available on both the Home Screen and the Lock Screen, and none of them are locked behind a subscription — the app's own listing puts it plainly as "every widget, free." There is also no limit on how many events you can track, so you can keep a birthday, a trip and a work deadline all counting down at the same time, each in its own widget if you'd rather not scroll through a list.

The list widget is worth a mention on its own: instead of dedicating a widget to every single event, it shows the next few in order, which tends to fit better on a Home Screen that already has other apps competing for space — the whole holiday season in one tile, for example, as in the Christmas countdown widget guide. The spotlight widget goes the other way, giving one event the most visual room, which suits whichever date matters most right now.

Live Activity: a countdown that runs on its own

A widget refreshes periodically, but a Live Activity keeps counting in real time on the Lock Screen and, on iPhones that have one, the Dynamic Island. From an event's own page you can pin it there and watch the days count down without opening the app at all. This is part of Countdown Pro, and it arrived in version 1.4.7, alongside a set of Halloween backdrops for the same release.

Because it lives in the Dynamic Island as well as the Lock Screen, a Live Activity tends to suit an event you're watching closely in its final stretch — the last day or two before a trip or an exam — rather than something you're tracking for months. A widget is the better fit for the long stretch beforehand, and you can switch to a Live Activity once the date is close.

Widget blank or not updating? What to do

First, what is normal. iOS, not the app, decides when a widget is redrawn, and it hands each widget a limited number of refreshes a day. A day count only needs one: Countdown gives iOS the next seven midnights in advance, so the number changes on its own around midnight without the app being opened. Anything you change in the app — a new date, a new event, a different backdrop — redraws the widgets right away. If a timed event counts in hours, minutes or seconds (a Countdown Pro format), the widget hands the ticking to iOS: in the final day, or from the start if the count leads with seconds, it becomes a clock that keeps running by itself. For a countdown that is also live in the Dynamic Island, use the Live Activity described above.

If your widget is out of step, match what it shows to the fix:

  1. It says “Nothing to count yet” but you have events. This can happen right after an app update, before Countdown has been opened once: the widget reads your events, and an update can change how they are stored. Open Countdown once and the widget fills back in. If it still says the same thing, remove the widget and add it again. A widget set to one particular event shows “Choose an event” in the same situation.
  2. It says “Choose an event”. The widget was set to one event and can't find it: the event was deleted or archived, or Countdown hasn't been opened since an update. Open Countdown once first. If the widget still says this, touch and hold it, tap Edit Widget and pick another event. If you deleted the event by mistake, restore it from the Trash in Countdown first.
  3. It is a day behind, or still shows yesterday's number. Turn off Low Power Mode, which can delay widget refreshes, and check that Background App Refresh is on for Countdown in Settings > General > Background App Refresh. Then unlock the phone and give it a minute.
  4. It is one day off after you travelled. Make sure Settings > General > Date & Time is set automatically. An all-day event in Countdown is a date, not a moment — Christmas stays 25 December in any time zone — but an event with a time set is a moment, so the number of days left can change by one when you cross time zones.
  5. It shows a different event from the one you picked, or keeps changing. The widget is probably in a stack. Touch and hold the stack, tap Edit Stack, and turn off Smart Rotate and Widget Suggestions.
  6. It is blank or grey after you reinstalled the app or set up a new iPhone. Your events come back from iCloud, which can take a few minutes. Open Countdown, wait until your list is there, then remove and add the widget if it is still empty.
  7. Every widget on the phone is stuck, not just Countdown. That points to iOS rather than the app. Restart the iPhone, then update iOS and Countdown.

Ending Countdown Pro never switches a widget off. Every widget is free, and a Pro backdrop you already put on a widget stays on it.

Set a reminder so the date doesn't sneak up on you

Widgets are easy to glance at, but a reminder makes sure you don't have to remember to look. Countdown lets you set a reminder for the day of the event itself, and as far ahead of it as you want — useful for a deadline you need to prepare for rather than just notice.

Events sync through iCloud with no account to create, so a widget you set up on one iPhone shows the same countdown on another, and there are no ads sitting alongside any of it. Questions people ask

Is there a free countdown widget for iPhone?

Yes. In Countdown Widget: Any Event every widget size is free on both the Home Screen and the Lock Screen, with unlimited events and no account. Pro unlocks the Pro backdrops, your own photos, the Pro typefaces and the Live Activity, but the widgets themselves are not part of it.

Can I put a countdown on my iPhone without an app?

Not one that counts down days. iOS has no built-in widget that shows the days left until a date you choose, so you need an app with a countdown widget. In Countdown every widget size is free.

Can I make a countdown widget extra large?

On iOS 27, yes: touch and hold the Countdown app icon or widget on the Home Screen and pick the extra large size. Earlier versions of iOS stop at large.

Can I add more than one countdown widget?

Yes. There is no limit on how many events Countdown can track, and you can place as many widgets as iOS allows on your Home Screen and Lock Screen.

Is the Lock Screen widget the same as Live Activity?

No. The Lock Screen widget refreshes like any other widget, while Live Activity is a separate, real-time display on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island, and it is part of Countdown Pro.

Why is my countdown widget stuck on the wrong number of days?

Check whether Low Power Mode is on, whether Background App Refresh is off for Countdown, and whether the iPhone's date and time are set automatically. A day count in Countdown changes on its own around midnight, and any edit in the app redraws the widget right away.

Do I need an account to sync my countdowns between devices?

No. Countdown syncs privately through iCloud with no account or sign-up required.

What happens to my events if I get a new iPhone?

iCloud sync is available with no account to make, and you can also import events from a calendar, a spreadsheet, or another countdown app.
